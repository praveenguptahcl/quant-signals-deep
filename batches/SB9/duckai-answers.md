**Kalman-filtered local-level fair value:**
Observed log-price y_t; latent fair value x_t. x_t = x_{t−1} + η_t, η_t~N(0,Q); y_t = x_t + ε_t, ε_t~N(0,R).
Prediction: x̂_{t|t−1} = x̂_{t−1|t−1}, P_{t|t−1} = P_{t−1|t−1} + Q.
Update: K_t = P_{t|t−1}/(P_{t|t−1}+R); x̂_{t|t} = x̂_{t|t−1} + K_t(y_t−x̂_{t|t−1}); P_{t|t} = (1−K_t)P_{t|t−1}.
Signal: e_t = y_t − x̂_{t|t} (residual) or s_t = y_t − x̂_{t|t−1} (next-period). Scale-free λ = Q/R.
Illustrative: Q/R = 10^{−4}–10^{−1} smooth; 10^{−1}–1 fast; R from bid–ask bounce/quote revisions/short-horizon var; Q from low-freq price-change variance minus noise; P_0 ∈ {R, 10R, 100R diffuse}. Larger Q/R → faster; larger R → trust observations less.

**PCA/eigenportfolio residual reversal:**
r_t ∈ R^N over rolling window W; standardize z_{i,t} = (r_{i,t}−r̄_i)/s_i. Σ = (1/(W−1))Z^⊤Z; Σv_j = λ_j v_j. m-factor reconstruction ẑ_t = Σ_{j=1}^m v_j(v_j^⊤z_t); residual u_t = z_t − ẑ_t; u_{i,t} = z_{i,t} − Σ_{j=1}^m v_{j,i}(v_j^⊤z_t). Signal = −u_{i,t}/σ̂_{u_i}, threshold |u/σ̂|>1.5–2.5. Factor form X = ΓF + ε [dokumen.pub]. Practical: W = 60–250d daily; 500–10,000 intraday; m = 1–5 (small universe) or 50–80% variance explained; residual half-life target 2–20 obs. NO look-ahead: use only info through t.

**Hasbrouck trade–quote VAR:**
m_t = (a_t+b_t)/2; Δm_t = m_t−m_{t−1}; q_t = signed trade size (+ buyer-initiated); x_t = (Δm_t,q_t)^⊤.
VAR(p): x_t = c + Σ_{ℓ=1}^p A_ℓ x_{t−ℓ} + u_t. Alternative: q_t = α_q + Σ(φ_ℓΔm_{t−ℓ} + ψ_ℓq_{t−ℓ}) + u_{q,t}.
IRF: IRF(H) = Σ_{h=0}^H Ψ_h e_q, Ψ_0=I, Ψ_h = Σ_{ℓ=1}^{min(h,p)} A_ℓΨ_{h−ℓ}. Long-run impact LRI = e_m^⊤(I−ΣA_ℓ)^{−1}e_q. Δm_∞ ≈ LRI·q for size q.
Illustrative: sampling 1s–5min; lags 5–30; size transform signed dollars/shares or sign(q)|q|^β; IRF horizon 50–500; add spread/depth if 2-var system inadequate. The informative component = UNEXPECTED trade innovation, not total volume [Studocu; repec.org].

**Fractional differentiation (fixed-width):**
(1−L)^d x_t = Σ_{k=0}^∞ w_k x_{t−k}; w_0=1, w_k = −w_{k−1}(d−k+1)/k. Fixed width L: x̃_t(d,L) = Σ_{k=0}^{L−1} w_k x_{t−k}. Truncate when |w_{L−1}| < τ (τ = 10^{−3}–10^{−5} illustrative). d=0 → x_t; d=1 → first differences. Practical: d = 0.1–0.4 memory-retaining; 0.4–0.8 stronger stationarization; d>1 usually unnecessary; L = 50–500; use log prices; choose smallest d passing stationarity test while retaining price correlation.

**Avellaneda–Stoikov reservation price + optimal spread:**
r(s,q,t) = s − qγσ²(T−t). Spread: δ_a+δ_b = γσ²(T−t) + (2/γ)ln(1+γ/κ); δ* = ½[γσ²(T−t) + (2/γ)ln(1+γ/κ)]; p_a = r+δ*, p_b = r−δ*. Arrival intensities λ_a = Ae^{−κ(p_a−s)}, λ_b = Ae^{−κ(s−p_b)}. Paper: Avellaneda–Stoikov 2008 limit-order-book model [repec.org; analytic-musings.com replication]. Illustrative (consistent price/time units): γ = 10^{−3}–1 per squared-price; κ = 0.1–10 per price; γ/κ = 10^{−3}–1; σ over same horizon as T−t; T−t = one execution cycle to one session; A from empirical fill counts (separate from γ,κ).

**(2) PCA residual reversal:** R_{i,t} = Σβ_{ij}F_{j,t} + R̃_{i,t}; residual often OU: dX_i = κ_i(μ_i−X_i)dt + σ_i dW_i. Avellaneda–Lee framework: PCA/eigenportfolios + residual construction + MR signals; widely reproduced (~1yr PCA window, shorter MR windows) [readthedocs]. Original paper commonly cited for attractive US historical performance — but later performance depends on: universe, short availability, turnover, borrow, vol targeting, rebalance freq, factor window, delistings, post-publication crowding. Recent OOS study: PCA strategies Sharpe ≈ 0.90–0.95 under 10bp round-trip, 1998–2021 [arXiv] — specific model/universe/rolling/construction, NOT universal. Public impl: ~6%/yr 2010–2019 with ~49% max drawdown [quantconnect] — positive mean CAN coexist with severe drawdown. **Crowding:** many participants estimate similar factors, buy same negative residuals, short same positive residuals, similar z thresholds, cut risk in same vol events → mechanical reversal on risk reduction. Residual trades NOT automatically "idiosyncratic alpha." 2007 quant unwind = crowded long-short strategies rapid losses when relationships broke [marquette.edu]. Risk control = speed of residual MR + goodness-of-fit, not blind residual trading. After-cost backtest: R_net = R_gross − Σc_i|Δw_i| − borrow − financing − impact. Stress: 2× spreads, ½ liquidity, borrow recalls, 1-day factor correlation jumps, forced liquidation, delayed execution, 2008-style shock. "Return usually low per unit capital; depends on leverage, shorting, diversification."
**(3) Dispersion:** σ_I² = Σw_i²σ_i² + 2Σ_{i<j}w_iw_jρ_{ij}σ_iσ_j. Trade = index vol vs constituent vol; exposed to implied index vs single-name, realized constituent vol, realized correlation. Common structure: index options carry LARGER vol risk premium than single-stock; dispersion harvests it but LOSES when correlations spike [quantpedia]. Competing interpretations: correlation-risk compensation vs option-market inefficiency; Deng finds evidence for inefficiency explanation (profitability changed after structural options-market changes) [ssrn]. **Documented edge: historical studies substantial Sharpe/excess returns; one summary 15.39% return 1996–2007 backtest [quantpedia] — historical, model-dependent, NOT expected return.** Real risks: vega, gamma/jump, skew, correlation, dividend/borrow, discrete-rebalance error, many-name transaction costs, surface model risk. **Capital intensity:** index leg + many constituent options + frequent delta hedging + short-option margin + stress collateral; net delta understates risk; correlation sensitivity ∂σ_I²/∂ρ_{ij} = 2w_iw_jσ_iσ_j. When correlations → 1 in selloff, index moves far more than constituent legs imply. **2008-style break:** profitable w/ diversified single-name moves + moderate correlations; LOSES when index vol jumps, correlations→1, index puts extremely expensive, constituent hedges illiquid, dealers/funds hedge simultaneously. "Precisely the wrong environment for a small account: margin expands, spreads widen, forced hedging at worst prices." Backtest must include: bid/ask execution, commissions, surface slippage, discrete hedging, margin, financing, stressed correlation. "Premium is compensation for correlation + liquidity risk."
**(4) Avellaneda–Stoikov MM:** r_t = S_t − q_tγσ²(T−t); λ(δ) = Ae^{−κδ}; exponential-utility inventory control [arXiv]. Theoretical trade-off (closer = more fills less spread/fill; farther = fewer more; imbalance → shift quotes to liquidate). **"Does NOT document that a live trader will earn the theoretical spread."** Original = primarily theoretical; simulations show positive P&L under own assumptions ≠ real OOS net. Restrictive assumptions: continuous prices, Brownian mid, Poisson arrivals, independent fills, stable vol, stable δ-fill relation, no queue priority, no latency, no impact, simplified fees. Realistic critiques: fills cluster, vol changes, modifications lose queue priority, prices jump, informed flow, costs reduce apparent capture [quantbeckman]. MM P&L = spread captured − adverse selection − inventory liquidation loss − fees/rebates mismatch − latency loss − queue loss − hedging cost. "A fill is not necessarily good news — buy-limit fill just before midprice falls: nominal spread outweighed by adverse selection." **Small-account reality:** insufficient inventory capacity, can't diversify, exchange minimums/fee tiers, limited inventory absorption, no colocation, poor queue priority, can't hedge rapidly, larger % fixed-fee impact, risk limits force quotes away exactly when inventory unbalanced. Objective = survival-adjusted net P&L: hard inventory limits, kill switches, vol widening, stale-quote cancel, conservative sizing > optimizing closed-form spread. Test: replay message-level books, conservative queue-aware fills, actual fees/rebates.
**(5) Hasbrouck information share:** For cointegrated venue prices, VECM → MA form; ψ = common-factor loading, Ω = innovation covariance, F = Cholesky: **IS_j = ([ψF]_j)²/(ψΩψ^⊤)** — proportion of permanent efficient-price innovation attributable to venue j [metricgate]. High IS = venue's unexpected moves account for larger fraction of common permanent innovation = WHERE price discovery occurs, NOT next-return forecast. NY Fed FX study: relative contribution changed across periods; higher-IS market NOT determined by size [newyorkfed]. **Not automatically a trading signal:** high IS can mean faster reaction to public info, more informed flow, better liquidity/lower latency, or other venues mechanically following. Doesn't tell: next-move direction, whether info already incorporated, regime stability of dominance, whether lead > latency+fees, survival after news controls. Limitations: (1) Cholesky ordering → order-dependent shares — report bounds, not one spurious number; (2) cointegration assumption; (3) sampling sensitivity (async/stale/over-fine sampling distorts covariance); (4) structural breaks; (5) endogeneity (venue responds to same news, not exploitable lead); (6) latency (lead may vanish before external trader accesses both markets). Trading use: choose venue to monitor first, route market-data subs, lead–lag features, cross-venue execution quality. Possible signal needs conditional extension: Δp_{B,t+h} = α + β·innovation_{A,t} + controls + ε, β tested OOS after latency+costs. **"The information-share statistic alone is descriptive, not predictive."**
