## Q-SB2-3: Documented evidence check — VERBATIM (key content)
**Executive conclusion:** ORB = evidence in selected implementations (esp. event-driven stocks-in-play), little verifiable evidence Crabel's originals survive modern costs broadly. RSI-2/IBS = strongest claims are practitioner/vendor backtests, not peer-reviewed; high hit rates coexist with severe trend-regime losses. HAR vs GARCH = strongest research support (forecasting, not trading alpha). Last-hour = measurable cross-sectional effects but sign not universal (continuation vs auction-reversal).
**(1) ORB after costs:** Crabel's book (Day Trading with Short-Term Price Patterns and Opening Range Breakout) — no longer readily available; no reproducible dataset/trade list/slippage/OOS → treat as documented framework, not verifiable modern evidence (ssrn.com). Holmberg–Lönnbark–Lundström: mechanical ORB rules on OHLC joint behavior; returns significantly >0, higher-than-fair-game success; abstract doesn't establish net-of-modern-spread/impact/borrow (repec.org). Zarattini–Barbon–Aziz: 7,000+ US stocks 2016–2023, 5-min ORBs (15/30/60-min variants); strongest = "Stocks in Play" filter (unusual activity/news); top-20 SIP portfolio: **>1,600% total net return, Sharpe 2.81, 36% annualized alpha AFTER their transaction-cost assumptions** (ssrn.com) — conditional strategy (opportunity filter, concentration, cost-model dependent, sample ends 2023), NOT evidence for generic 500-stock ORB. Survives modern spreads? Sometimes — plausible with: news/earnings catalyst, very high rel vol, small spread vs expected move, limit/stop execution at breakout, small size vs liquidity, avoiding marginal breaks/range days. Vulnerable: trade-everything-every-day, too-short OR (false breaks), late entries, small-cap/wide-spread, bar high/low treated as executable, fixed-bps slippage. True test: R_net = R_gross − spread − slippage − impact − fees − borrow.
**(2) RSI-2/IBS:** classic rules via Connors-style practitioner research; IBS=(C−L)/(H−L). "70–90% win" claims = books/newsletters/proprietary, NOT one peer-reviewed reproducible study (Connors Research material presents IBS as concept, no accepted published hit-rate benchmark — alvarezquanttrading.com). Dependencies: ETF vs single stocks, long-only vs L/S, threshold, trend filter, hold period, close vs next-open entry, survivorship, spread+impact. Pattern: very high win rate oversold-longs in broad indices/ETFs; lower in single names; better with trend filter; bigger avg losses in persistent declines. **Expectancy math (VERIFIED): 75% win, +0.25% avg win, −1.2% avg loss → 0.75(0.0025)−0.25(0.012) = −0.001125 = −0.1125%/trade before costs.** Failure regimes: persistent bear trends; vol cascades (2nd/3rd leg); gap-driven (next-open slippage); single-name news; liquidity shocks; crowded mean reversion. Defense: regime filter (index > long MA), capped size, limited re-entries. = conditional short-horizon reversal signals, not standalone strategies.
**(3) HAR vs GARCH:** HAR: RV_{t+1}=β_0+β_d RV_t+β_w RV_t(5)+β_m RV_t(22)+ε; GARCH from lagged squared returns + lagged cond var. Edge = realized vol's multiscale persistence, omitted by daily-return GARCH. Vortelinos: 7 US markets (spot eq, FX, ETFs, eq-index futs, Tsy-bond futs, energy, commodity options) — nonlinear models and GARCH NOT more accurate than HAR overall; persistence too important to omit (repec.org). Review: HAR-type beat GARCH in several settings; S&P 500 RV Feb-2008–Feb-2015 incl. 2008 crisis; US stocks 1965–2020 more robust to microstructure noise (Springer). Recent OOS: HAR family lower MSE/MAE/HMSE/HMAE/QLIKE than GARCH, passed model-confidence-set more often — dataset-specific (arXiv). Cost status: BEFORE-cost forecast accuracy (MSE/MAE/QLIKE/HMSE/HMAE/OOS R²/MCS) — lower loss ≠ tradable edge; economic value needs vol-targeting/sizing/hedging/risk-limit/turnover-utility proof. Breaks: microstructure noise contamination; too-high sampling w/o correction; structural breaks; horizon ≠ (1,5,22) design; jump-dominated; weak persistence; small samples; crisis regime shifts. Extensions: jump-HAR (longer horizons), realized semivariance, log-HAR, threshold HAR, regime-switching, time-varying coeffs. = strongest statistical case here, but forecasting gain, not net trading alpha.
**(4) End-of-day/last-hour/auction:** not one effect — same-day continuation, cross-sectional last-30/60-min momentum, auction-imbalance continuation, EOD pressure→next-day reversal, overnight drift. Sign differs by definition/hold. Last-30-min cross-sectional REVERSAL: NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq 1993–2019, ex-micro/<$5: sharp reversals final 30 min esp. intraday losers; attributed to price pressure (alphaarchitect.com) — hypothetical portfolio, before full costs. Auction impact (NYSE 2023, Russell 1000): ~2.4% of CADV → immediate impact ≈ **0.34× daily avg spread**; drift to auction price ≈ **0.6–1.7× spread** by size/timing; impact 0.3× spread at 3:55–3:56 → 0.4× at 3:59–4:00. Continuation vs reversal: winners-into-close can = index-rebalance/ETF/mutual-fund/options/retail-deadline flows (apparent momentum) → next-day reversal if temporary pressure. Overnight: 1% ADV trade ≈ **40bp round-trip impact** under sqrt model at 2% daily vol; low-cost overnight versions weakened in recent years (elmwealth.com). Breaks: index reconstitution, triple-witching/expiry, earnings, macro days, selloffs, small-cap/low-ADV, big rebalances, crowded close signals, auction-rule changes.
